19907Practical Significance with Regard to Land Use
19908The County Powers Act provides a meaningful, but bounded, toolkit for Tennessee counties. Counties
19909that have not adopted comprehensive zoning under the County Zoning Act (T.C.A. § 13-7-101 to -119)
19910often rely on § 5-1-118(c) police powers to regulate land uses in unincorporated areas. However, as the
19911Tinsley Properties decision makes clear, counties must exercise caution if a resolution effectively
19912functions as a zoning ordinance, and particularly if it seeks to divide the county into districts and/or to
19913regulate land use by location, it must comply with the County Zoning Act's procedural requirements,
19914regardless of the legal authority the county invokes.
19915​
19916​
19917Counties considering land-use regulations under the County Powers Act should:
19918 • Ensure regulations are truly general police-power measures (applying uniformly) rather than
19919 location-based land-use controls. A potentially defensible example would be a countywide noise
19920 ordinance;
19921 • Consult with legal counsel to assess whether a proposed resolution would satisfy the
19922 Tinsley/Cherokee Country Club substantial effects test;
19923 • Consider whether formal adoption of zoning under the County Zoning Act would provide a more
19924 defensible regulatory framework;
19925 • Be mindful of the statutory exemptions for state-regulated industries and the protection of

19927 • Remember that blanket prohibitions of otherwise legal uses may be subject to intense legal

19932The County Powers Act provides Tennessee counties with a meaningful set of tools to address local
19933needs ranging from contracting and public utilities to law enforcement cooperation and, where
19934authorized by supermajority vote, general police powers. However, the Act has clear boundaries. It does
