1 SUBSTITUTE ORDINANCE NO. 5982
2AN ORDINANCE DECLARING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE
3ACCEPTANCE, PROCESSING, APPROVAL, AND ISSUANCE OF ZONING, LAND
4USE, BUILDING, AND OTHER ASSOCIATED INFRASTRUCTURE PERMITS FOR
5DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENTS WITHIN THE CITY OF MEMPHIS
6​
7WHEREAS, pursuant to Tennessee Code Annotated § 13-7-201, the Memphis City Council
8possesses the authority to regulate land use, zoning, and development to protect the public
9health, safety, and welfare of its citizens; and
10​
11WHEREAS, there has been substantial growth nationwide and throughout Tennessee in the
12development of large-scale data centers and high-density computing facilities supporting cloud
13computing, artificial intelligence, digital storage, cryptocurrency operations, and other advanced
14computing technologies; and
15​
16WHEREAS, modern data centers differ significantly from traditional commercial and industrial
17developments because they may require exceptionally high electrical loads, extensive backup
18generation systems, electrical substations, battery storage systems, mechanical cooling
19equipment, water resources, telecommunications infrastructure, and continuous twenty-four-hour
20operations; and
21​
22WHEREAS, such facilities may generate impacts relating to electrical infrastructure capacity,
23water consumption, wastewater management, stormwater runoff, emergency services, traffic,
24lighting, vibration, noise, air quality, fuel storage, public utilities, and surrounding
25neighborhoods; and

Lines 26–50

26​
27WHEREAS, the Memphis and Shelby County Unified Development Code does not presently
28classify or comprehensively regulate data center developments as a distinct land use and
29therefore does not specifically address their unique operational characteristics or potential
30community impacts; and
31​
32WHEREAS, additional study is necessary before permitting additional data center developments
33to ensure that appropriate zoning classifications, siting standards, buffering requirements,
34operational standards, utility protections, environmental safeguards, and community protections
35are established; and
36​
37WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium should serve as a defined planning and regulatory-
38development period and not as an indefinite or blanket prohibition, and should culminate in a
39transparent framework identifying where qualifying facilities may appropriately be located, what
40information applicants must submit, how proposed projects will be evaluated regardless of
41energy source, and how affected communities will participate in the process; and
42​
43WHEREAS, meaningful public participation should occur before the City designates preferred
44areas for future data center development so that neighborhood impacts, infrastructure needs,
45environmental concerns, economic opportunities, and community priorities may inform the final
46siting and regulatory framework; and
47WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium is necessary to prevent applications from vesting under
48existing regulations while the City undertakes the community-engagement, study, siting, and
49permanent-regulation process established by this Ordinance.

54SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS
55​
56For purposes of this Ordinance, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:
57​
58Affiliated Facility means a facility, phase, parcel, project entity, tenant, or power-supply
59arrangement under common ownership or control, developed pursuant to a common plan,
60sharing material infrastructure, or located on contiguous or proximate parcels.
61​
62Data Center means any building, structure, campus, or facility primarily designed for housing,
63operating, or maintaining computer servers, data storage equipment, networking equipment,
64telecommunications infrastructure, or other electronic systems used for processing, transmitting,
65or storing digital information, together with all associated cooling systems, backup generators,
66substations, battery storage, mechanical equipment, and supporting infrastructure.
67High-Density Computing Facility means any facility engaged primarily in artificial intelligence
68processing, cryptocurrency mining, commercial server farming, or other computing activities
69requiring substantial electrical demand or specialized computing equipment.

71Maximum Site Electrical Demand or MSED means the greatest projected coincident electrical

73regardless of whether supplied by Memphis Light, Gas and Water (“MLGW”), a TVA-connected
74service, an independent power producer, behind-the-meter generation, onsite generation, storage,
75or any combination thereof, measured before subtracting onsite generation or storage.

126SECTION 3. EXEMPTIONS
127The moratorium established by this Ordinance shall not apply to:
128​
129A. Existing lawfully operating Data Centers or High-Density Computing Facilities that do not
130expand their building footprint, electrical demand, utility capacity, or operational intensity;
131​
132B. Applications legally vested pursuant to applicable state or local law prior to the introduction
133of this Ordinance;
134​
135C. Routine maintenance, repair, replacement, or modernization of an existing facility that does
136not materially increase operational impacts;
137​
138D. Accessory Computing Uses. Computer servers, data storage equipment, networking
139equipment, telecommunications equipment, or related computing infrastructure that is accessory
140or incidental to another lawful principal use of property and primarily serves the operational
141needs of such principal use. This exclusion includes, without limitation, computing infrastructure
142accessory to hospitals and healthcare facilities, financial institutions, educational institutions,
143governmental facilities, offices, retail establishments, manufacturing facilities,
144telecommunications facilities, and similar principal uses; and
145​
146E. A waiver granted by the Memphis City Council upon written findings that strict application of
147the moratorium would deny an applicant all economically viable use of the property. An
148applicant may initiate the waiver process through the Memphis and Shelby County Division of
149Planning and Development utilizing the applicable appeal process to the Council.
