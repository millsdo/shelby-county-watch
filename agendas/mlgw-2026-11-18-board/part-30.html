11772Memphis Sand Aquifer
11773​
11774 The City possesses some of the highest-quality drinking water in the United States and stands among
11775a few select cities boasting a water supply sourced entirely from groundwater. The Memphis Sand Aquifer

Lines 11776–11800

11776system is the primary water source for the City, is one of the City’s greatest natural resources, and provides an
11777abundant supply of pure water. On an average day, MLGW pumps 120 million gallons of water from the
11778Memphis Sand Aquifer that is delivered to 10 pumping stations around the City. The water is then aerated,
11779filtered, treated and distributed across the Service Area through its distribution mains for consumption by its
11780residential, commercial and industrial customer base.
11781​
11782 The Memphis Sand Aquifer system is comprised of four water-bearing layers of sand and gravel are
11783situated from 50 to 2,600 feet below the City’s ground surface. The Memphis Sand Aquifer system contains
11784alternating layers of water bearing sand and impermeable clay, which stretches from the Tennessee River to
11785the Arkansas Black River. Other aquifers located beneath the Memphis Sand Aquifer are the Fort Pillow
11786Sand, McNairy Sand and Coffee Sand. Water in each aquifer varies slightly in quality, mineral content and
11787taste. The Water Division’s water supply is drawn from the second layer of sand at depths that range from
11788350 to 1,100 feet. The City is one of the largest city in the world to rely solely on Artesian Wells for its water
11789supply. The Water Division focuses on the supply, treatment, transmission, and distribution of water from the
11790Memphis Sand Aquifer system.
