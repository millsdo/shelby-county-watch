811 MLGW OVERVIEW
812​
813Memphis Light, Gas and Water is the nation’s largest three-service public utility, providing electric,
814natural gas and water services throughout Memphis and Shelby County. Established in 1939, MLGW
815supports the region’s quality of life and economic vitality by delivering essential utility services to
816hundreds of thousands of residential, commercial and industrial customers.
817​
818MLGW receives electricity from the Tennessee Valley Authority and operates three utility systems
819through one customer-facing organization. This integrated structure gives customers one bill, one
820service provider and one organization responsible for supporting the infrastructure that powers homes,
821businesses and community growth.
822​
823A Legacy of Public Service
824MLGW’s roots extend to 1852 with the formation of the Memphis Gas Light Company. Memphis
825established municipal ownership of its water system in 1902, and the modern Memphis Light, Gas and

826Water Division was formed in 1939 by bringing electric, gas and water services together under one
827public utility. That legacy continues through MLGW’s commitment to reliable service, affordability and
828responsible stewardship.
829​
830Municipal Ownership and Expansion
831In 1902, the city of Memphis purchased The Artesian Water Company, establishing the Memphis
832Artesian Water Department, the first city-owned utility. This move marked the beginning of municipal
833ownership and control over essential services. The city’s commitment to providing reliable utilities
834continued to grow, culminating in the formation of MLGW in 1939. This was achieved through the
835acquisition of the privately-owned Memphis Power and Light, consolidating electricity, gas and water
836services under one entity.
837​
838Community Impact
839MLGW’s role extends beyond providing essential utility services. Through community partnerships,
840employee volunteerism, customer assistance programs and energy education, MLGW works to
841strengthen the quality of life throughout Memphis and Shelby County.
842​
843Modernizing the Grid
844MLGW is modernizing the electric grid through investments in advanced communications, automation
845and technologies that improve system visibility. These improvements will strengthen resiliency, support
846faster restoration and prepare the grid for the community’s changing energy needs.

944How to Read the Budget
945​
946INTRODUCTION
947We are pleased to submit the 2027 Budget for Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division (MLGW) as
948required by Memphis City Ordinance #3509. This Budget has been prepared in compliance with this
949ordinance, which requires conformity to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC), the
950regulations of any other applicable regulatory body, and in accordance with the provisions of the

Lines 951–975

951bond resolutions approved by the Memphis City Council.
952​
953Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division’s accounting and financial practices conform to
954Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP), under the rules and regulations of the
955Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB) and the Financial Accounting Standards
956Board (FASB).
957​
958Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division was created by an amendment to the City Charter,
959adopted March 9, 1939. MLGW operates three separate utilities, as divisions, providing electricity
960and gas in the City and Shelby County. Water service is provided by MLGW in the City and,
961together with other municipal systems, in Shelby County. Each division operates as a separate
962entity for accounting and financial purposes pursuant to the City Charter. For economic reasons,
963activities common to all three divisions are administered jointly and costs are prorated among the
964divisions. The 2027 operating and capital budgets are developed simultaneously and are presented
965together in a combined budget. In this manner, MLGW can accomplish personnel planning and
966allocate resources to ensure the achievement of each strategic area of focus.
967​
968THE BUDGET PROCESS
969Memphis Light, Gas and Water Division develops the operating and capital expenditure budgets
970annually to coincide with its fiscal year, which extends from January 1 to December 31. The budget
971process begins early in the year that precedes the fiscal year for which the budget will take effect.
972The entire process continues for several months until final approval is obtained by the Memphis City
973Council.
974​
975At the onset of the process, a budget schedule is created to establish the critical deadlines for

Lines 976–1000

976activities necessary in producing the final approved budget document. Some of the budget
977calendar events include planning meetings, multiple budget training sessions, capital project
978budget meetings and equipment budget meetings, all of which are completed before the end of
979June.
980​
981During July through August, requests for the upcoming budget year are submitted by organization
982supervisors and managers. The vice presidents are then responsible for reviewing the proposed
983budget and making necessary adjustments.
984​
985The proposed budget goes through several stages of formal review and approval before being
986finalized. The initial level of approval occurs during mid-August through early October by the
987members of MLGW’s executive staff. A preliminary budget document is subsequently
988developed and presented to the MLGW Board of Commissioners for review and approval in
989October, with the final approval by the Memphis City Council scheduled for November.
