92INTRODUCTION:
93Staff requests approval of the First Amendment to Contract GS-25-01552 with Freese and Nichols, Inc.
94(FNI), adding a code diagnostic to the Collierville 2050 Comprehensive Plan project for a lump sum fee
95of $65,000.00. The amendment raises the total contract amount from $500,000.00 to $565,000.00.
96​
97The diagnostic will identify the conflicts and major changes needed to the Town's zoning and land
98development regulations so that the 2050 Comprehensive Plan can be implemented. It is a planning-
99level review and recommendation report, not a rewrite of the Town's codes.
100​

Lines 101–125

101BACKGROUND:
102On September 22, 2025, the Town entered into a Professional Services Agreement with Freese and
103Nichols, Inc., a Texas corporation, to prepare the Collierville 2050 Comprehensive Plan. The original
104agreement totaled $500,000.00 and is recorded as Contract GS-25-01552.
105​
106While a comprehensive plan sets the Town's long-range vision, the zoning ordinance and subdivision
107regulations are the tools that put it into effect. As the 2050 Plan advances, staff has identified the need
108to confirm how well the Town's current regulations align with the Plan's goals and strategies, and where
109they do not. FNI submitted a proposal dated October 12, 2026, to perform that review as a code
110diagnostic, and the proposal is attached to the amendment as Exhibit A.
111​
112Because the work is an addition to the scope of the existing agreement, it is processed as a First
113Amendment to Contract rather than a new procurement. The amendment amends Section 1.00, Scope of
114Agreement, and ratifies all other terms of the original agreement.
115​
116DISCUSSION:
117FNI will review the Town's adopted plans and policies (including the Comprehensive Plan 2050, Major
118Road Plan, Greenbelt Master Plan, and Tree Policy), the zoning regulations in Title 15, Chapter 151 of
119the Collierville Code of Ordinances, the subdivision regulations (last updated May 1, 2024), and the
120Historic District and Design Review Guidelines. The review covers the corporate limits and the reserve
121areas. FNI will compare current practice against best practices, the Plan's goals, and recent development
122trends, using recent development applications provided by staff and input from code users.
123​
124FNI will lead up to six hours of in-person or virtual stakeholder meetings with code users such as staff,
125developers, designers, architects, engineers, homebuilders, and attorneys. These meetings are meant to

Lines 126–150

126surface how the current standards shape the built environment.
127​
128The product is a concise diagnostic report listing recommended major edits, document structure
129changes, and process changes to guide a future code update. The final draft will include the detailed
130analysis, the major modifications needed, and a proposed table of contents for the updated zoning and
131land development regulations. FNI will then issue an "as approved" final report reflecting any changes
132made during the BMA review.
133​
134The scope includes four meetings:
1351. Project initiation call with staff
1362. Stakeholder meeting(s), within the six hours above
1373. Staff feedback call on the draft report, up to two hours
1384. One presentation to the Mayor and Board of Aldermen
139​
140FNI is to complete the services within eight months of commencing work.
141​
142STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends approval of the first amendment to the
143Comprehensive Plan contract, authorizing Freese & Nichols to conduct a Code Diagnostic.

239BACKGROUND:

246satisfactorily and has proven to be responsive to our needs.
247​
248The Town generates approximately 240,000 utility bills and notices annually. When necessary, Town
249communications or periodic inserts such as the Budget in Brief and the Water Quality Report can be
250inserted with utility bills to save postage costs.
