The big week is Tuesday, Nov 10. The Memphis City Council holds third and final readings on three data center ordinances: zoning districts for data centers (Ord. 5987), a siting framework (Ord. 5988) and Councilman Spinosa's replacement for the blanket moratorium (Ord. 5985). It also votes to spend $3,259,282 in FY2026 data center tax revenue on nine neighborhood programs near the Paul R. Lowery Rd AI site. The Bartlett Board of Mayor and Aldermen takes up a $5,683,893.34 sole-source pipe purchase for the Fletcher Creek sewer tank, a rewritten YMCA lease and a new Quail Ridge golf deal. MSCS committees and the board work session take up 16 school contracts over $10,000 and a $257 million budget amendment. The County Commission has no meetings this week. Its committees meet next on Wed Nov 18 and the full Commission on Mon Nov 23. Arlington: nothing new posted. The Design Review Committee meets Tue Nov 10 with no agenda posted yet.

Data centers: Memphis Council final votes on zoning, siting and the moratorium replacement

MemphisMemphis City CouncilTue Nov 10, 4:00 PM

“23. ORDINANCE to Adopt Zoning Amendments to the Unified Development Code Relative to Data Centers, up for THIRD and FINAL reading. Ordinance No. 5987”
Show full quotes
“23. ORDINANCE to Adopt Zoning Amendments to the Unified Development Code Relative to Data Centers, up for THIRD and FINAL reading. Ordinance No. 5987”
“24. ORDINANCE ... to Establish a Land-Use and Siting Framework for Data Centers and other Resource-Intensive Uses Within the City of Memphis, up for THIRD and FINAL reading. Ordinance No. 5988”
“25. ORDINANCE To Establish A Strategic Large-Load Development And Digital Infrastructure District Program; To Replace A Blanket Data-Center Moratorium With Source-Neutral, Tiered, And Timed Review ... Ordinance No. 5985 is sponsored by Councilman Spinosa. (Held from 10/6; 10/20)”
Agenda
Details
  • Ord. 5985 sets a "recurring fiscal floor." No incentive may cut the City payment on a data center's core computing equipment "below one hundred percent of the ad valorem tax that would be due if the property were privately owned and fully taxable." Land pays 100% and support equipment at least 50%, so these projects keep paying real taxes.
  • Ord. 5985 takes effect only after an MLGW large-load service policy is in place, and at that moment the pending moratorium ends ("There shall be no interval in which the moratorium is absent").
  • Same day: the Council's Planning and Zoning committee (2:15 PM) gets a DPD "Update on Data Center Zoning progress," and the Executive Session (2:45 PM) discusses Ord. 5989. The last data center work session, "Final Review and Next Steps," is Mon Nov 9, 1:00 PM (city calendar listing; no agenda posted).
  • Item 32, a Cooper-Sutton resolution "requesting comprehensive data center standards," is also back (held from 10/6 and 10/20).
Spending & grants

Data centers: $3,259,282 in data center tax revenue for nine neighborhood programs

MemphisMemphis City CouncilTue Nov 10, 4:00 PM

“30. RESOLUTION to Allocate and Appropriate ... ($3,259,282.00) in Data Center Revenues Collected During FY2026 to Nine Projects and Programs, in accordance with Community Benefit Ordinance Number 5953.”
Agenda
Details
  • The money is the 25% set-aside of City property taxes from the AI property on Paul R. Lowery Rd, to be spent within five miles of the site. Data centers help the County's and City's tax base.
  • The split, verified to add up to $3,259,282: home repair $2,000,000; litter removal jobs $250,000; board-up and demolition $250,000; blight cleanup $150,000; Memphis City Beautiful $25,000; environmental education $100,000; air conditioners, fans and filters $125,000; grants to non-governmental organizations $359,282.
  • Also on consent: Ord. 5989, second reading, a framework for a nonprofit "Community Benefit Agency" to receive data center community-benefit funding. Its text says the agency "shall not exercise governmental powers, impose mandatory assessments, collect municipal taxes."
