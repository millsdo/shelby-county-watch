How to read this report

Every quote is copied from the posted agenda or packet unless marked as a news report. "Confirmed" means the body's own posting gives the date and time; "Expected" means the date comes from a calendar, notice, or regular schedule only. Red flags are leads for review, not accusations.

What changed since the Fri Oct 23 morning check: The full County Commission agenda for Mon Oct 26 posted Fri Oct 23 at 3:32 PM. It carries the County's data center moratorium (2nd reading, sent down without a committee recommendation) and a County version of data center development standards (1st reading, amended in committee), the household hazardous waste agreements with Arlington and the other six cities, a $61,000 exception to the County's own grant moratorium for Thanksgiving meals, and two Judicial Commissioner appointments with the names left blank. The Commission then adjourns to Mon Nov 23. Bartlett canceled its Oct 27 Board of Mayor and Aldermen meeting; the 6:00 PM work session (Bartlett Performing Arts and Conference Center survey) stays on. Millington's Industrial Development Board posted a Mon Oct 26 agenda (MLGW presentation, 52 acres on Wilkinsville Rd). New on calendars: a County Investment Committee discussion meeting (Tue Oct 27, 2:30 PM) and the Memphis and Shelby County Film and Television Commission board (Wed Oct 28, 9:00 AM). The text of the data center ordinance the Memphis Council passed Oct 20 (substitute Ord. 5986, with a 135-day pause) is now on the city's website. The Memphis Council does not meet this week or on Nov 3; Nov 3 has two committees, and the next full Council is Tue Nov 10. Arlington's town document library and calendar were re-checked in a browser this evening: no new town agendas and no town meetings this week (the Board of Mayor and Aldermen meets Mon Nov 2).

County data center moratorium (2nd reading) and County data center standards (1st reading)

CountyShelby County Board of CommissionersMon Oct 26

“ORDINANCE – SECOND READING – Ordinance declaring a Temporary Moratorium, not to exceed twelve (12) months, on “permits” … for Data Center developments within the City of Memphis, Shelby County Government Board of Commissioners of…”
Show full quotes
“ORDINANCE – SECOND READING – Ordinance declaring a Temporary Moratorium, not to exceed twelve (12) months, on “permits” … for Data Center developments within the City of Memphis, Shelby County Government Board of Commissioners of Tennessee, in coordination with and joining with the City Council of the City of Memphis, in declaring a Temporary Moratorium.”
“ORDINANCE - FIRST READING – … to establish regulations for the siting, initial development, construction, expansion, alteration, and operation of large-scale Date Centers; … requiring progressive use of renewable and non-carbon electricity; establishing a schedule for the phase-out of Carbon-Based Electricity…”
Agenda
Details
  • Item 29 (moratorium; Brooks, Sugarmon) was "SENT DOWN WITHOUT RECOMMENDATION" by the Oct 21 committee. It says it joins a City moratorium, but the City Council voted that moratorium down on Oct 20 (6-6, per news reports) and passed a 135-day pause instead.
  • Item 28 (standards) was "AMENDED IN OCTOBER 21, 2026 COMMITTEE MEETING"; sponsors are now Huett-Garcia, Streeter, Rudd and Sugarmon. The amended text is not posted. Its title matches the original Memphis Ord. 5986 title; the Memphis version that passed keeps that title but its body is a study-and-draft framework plus the 135-day pause (see the Memphis item below).
  • Data centers add to the County's tax base. The City set aside $3,259,282 in FY2026 data center revenue, its 25% share of City property taxes from one AI property on Paul R. Lowery Rd.

Agenda
Details

  • Background, now posted: substitute Ord. 5986 (sponsors Logan and Warren), passed Oct 20 per news reports, directs the planning division to bring a code and siting analysis within 45 days and draft permanent data center rules within 90 days, and sets a "TEMPORARY ONE HUNDRED THIRTY-FIVE (135) DAY PAUSE ON NEW DATA-CENTER APPLICATIONS AND DEVELOPMENT APPROVALS." The pause "shall not be interpreted as a permanent prohibition upon data-center development." Despite its long title, the posted body has no cooling-system or carbon phase-out mandates.
