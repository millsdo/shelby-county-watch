Data centers

Every data center item across all bodies. Data centers add to the local tax base; the items below are reported neutrally.

County: data center standards pass first reading, 9-4 (new since Sunday)

Body: Shelby County Board of Commissioners · Date: Mon Nov 23, 3:00 PM (held) · Agenda: https://agenda.shelbycountytn.gov/OnBaseAgendaOnline/Meetings/ViewMeeting?id=3721&doctype=1

"ORDINANCE - FIRST READING –Ordinance amending Ordinance No. 397, known as the Memphis and Shelby County Unified Development Code, to establish regulations for the siting ... of large-scale Data Centers" - Item 44 passed first reading 9 ayes, 4 noes, after about 30 minutes of discussion and public comment (Action News 5, WREG, Yahoo News/Jeremiah Hall, Nov 23). The named roll call has not been published; OnBase shows no minutes or action summary yet. - The ordinance needs two more readings, a two-thirds vote of the membership and Land Use Control Board approval. The Daily Memphian reported the draft "is expected to change next month as the Shelby County Board of Commissioners moves toward a data center moratorium." Commissioner Huett-Garcia told WREG she is working with the County Attorney on enforcement language, fees and fines. Next Commission meeting: Mon Dec 14, 3:00 PM.

Memphis: data center ordinances at the Nov 24 Council (results unconfirmed)

Body: Memphis City Council · Date: Tue Nov 24, 4:00 PM (held) · Agenda: https://memphistn.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/REVISED-AG-11-24-2026.pdf - Ord. 5989 (Warren), third and final reading, creates the framework for a nonprofit data center Community Benefit Agency. Memphis Report said the audio was not clear enough to report a result, and no minutes are posted. - Ord. 5991 (Cooper-Sutton), first reading, adds the 5C data center site (46.88 acres on Getwell Road) to the community benefits ordinance. New since Sunday: the Council posted the full redline of Ord. 5953 for the Executive session. It sets aside an amount equal to "25% of the real and personal property tax revenues collected by the City" on the listed AI sites, for public purposes within five miles. The first-reading result was not clearly reported. - Redline: https://memphistn.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Exec-Full-Ord.-5953-Redline-Cooper-Sutton.pdf

MLGW

  • Changed: rate votes delayed to a Dec 8 public hearing. At the Nov 24 Council, after the ratemaking items were added to the agenda, the Council set the public hearing for Tue Dec 8 and held the rate resolutions until then (Memphis Report, FOX13, Daily Memphian, Commercial Appeal). Councilman JB Smiley Jr. moved the delay, saying the public needed more notice (Daily Memphian). The full roll call is unconfirmed until minutes are posted.
  • What is pending, per the committee resolutions: gas "increase in gas revenues of 12.0%" in 2027, then 11.0% in 2028 and 11.0% in 2029; water "increase in water revenues of 16.0%" in each of 2027, 2028 and 2029; the W-15 and W-16 data center water rates; a new EV charging rate; and a change to the electric fuel cost rider "which will allow for recovery of all purchased power costs." MLGW's CEO told WREG the average household would pay "just a little under $27" more a month after three years.
  • Water Utility Relief Program passed (item 24, Cooper-Sutton abstaining, per the stream as reported by Memphis Report): $4 million over three fiscal years for low-income water bill help, with $1,333,000 in FY2027 from a forecast surplus in local sales tax and business tax ($666,500 each).
  • MLGW Board next meets Wed Dec 2, 8:30 AM (no agenda yet).
