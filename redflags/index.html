Red flags

These are automated leads for review, not accusations. Each card quotes agenda text that matched a pattern sometimes associated with weak process.
More about how to read these

Examples are sole-source buys, after-the-fact approvals, or large items on a consent agenda. Many have ordinary explanations. Read the full item in the official document, and ask the body or staff before drawing conclusions. Dollar figures show the requested amount and, where the document gives one, the contract total. What each rule looks for

No-bid / sole-source / emergency procurement
Purchases made without normal competitive bidding (sole source, emergency purchase, bid waiver, piggyback on another agency's contract) skip the usual price competition and deserve a look at the justification.
After-the-fact approval / ratification
Approving spending or contracts after the money was committed or the work was done bypasses the body's prior approval.
Large contract increase / ceiling raise
Amendments, change orders, or not-to-exceed increases that grow a contract by more than 25% can signal scope creep or an award that was underpriced to win.
Possible bid splitting (amounts just under thresholds)
The same vendor receiving several amounts just below common approval/bid thresholds ($25k, $50k, $100k) can indicate purchases split to avoid bidding or board approval.
Vendor with many awards / large total
Vendors appearing on many items or across several bodies, aggregated by name. Informational, for checking concentration of public money.
Conflict of interest / recusal / related party
Language about conflicts, recusals, related parties, or officials' employees and family members marks items where personal interests may be involved.
Blank names or amounts
Resolutions or appointments with blank names/amounts ("_____", TBD) are voted on before the public can see the key facts.
Late additions, rule suspensions, repeated deferrals
Walk-on items, suspension of rules, emergency ordinances, and items held over many times reduce public notice or signal contested items.
Executive / closed session
Closed sessions are limited under the Tennessee Open Meetings Act (generally attorney-client discussion of pending litigation). Sessions with no stated legal basis deserve a question.
Settlements, severance, outside counsel, consultants, lobbyists
Legal settlements, severance/separation payments, and hiring outside counsel, consultants or lobbyists are discretionary payments worth checking for amount and justification.
Land deals: nominal price, below appraisal, eminent domain, PILOT/TIF
Transfers of public property for nominal value or below appraisal, condemnations, land-bank transfers, and PILOT/TIF tax incentives move public value to private parties.
Large dollar item on consent agenda
Consent agendas are approved in one vote without discussion. Items over $1 million there get less scrutiny.
School purchase over $10,000
School board items that approve a purchase, contract, bid award, service contract, renewal, or equipment buy over $10,000 (Lee, Oct 30, 2026). Shown so school spending gets a look at vendor, price and how it was bought; most are routine. Budget amendments, revenue, grants/donations accepted, property sales, policy thresholds, salary schedules and totals repeated in prior minutes are excluded.
Grants to nonprofits (check for ties to officials)
Discretionary grants to nonprofits and community organizations should be checked manually for board or family ties to the officials sponsoring them.

Known false positives suppressed

  • Blank names or amounts: blank not in an appointment/resolution/amount context (41 lines)
  • Blank names or amounts: ordinance/resolution number blanks and empty form fields (28 lines)
  • School purchase over $10,000: revenue, grant, donation or property sale, not a purchase (14 lines)
  • Blank names or amounts: signature/form-fill blanks in packet documents (13 lines)
  • Land deals: nominal price, below appraisal, eminent domain, PILOT/TIF: PILOT mentioned inside ordinance definitions or rules (data-center legislation), not a PILOT approval (12 lines)
  • School purchase over $10,000: budget amendment / account move, not a purchase (4 lines)
